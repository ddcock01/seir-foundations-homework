Databases
When I first came in contact with databases, I thought they were fancy spreadsheets. Actually, they're data storage methods for applications. However,
databases, become more than just for storing data once you begin to understand that you can also manage, order, and retrieve data from applications in
useful ways. Databases come in 2 different flavors; Relational/Structured Query Language (SQL) or non-relational/non-SQL. Relational Databases have your
traditional spreadsheet set up; a table with columns and rows. Relational databases strictly adhere to ADCID (Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability)
Schema-less, non-relational, non-SQL databases store enformation in different formats that include; key value pairs, JSON documents, or graph nodes.
Non-Relational databases are more flexible or have semi-structured or unstructured data.

COBOL Program
Apparently COBOL stands for Common Business-Oriented Language, and is one of the oldest programming languages still used today.
The language was created back in 1959 for busines, finance, and administrative systems, like banking. It also mentioned key characterestics
which included: English-Like Syntax, Fixed-Width Records, Batch Processing & Ledger Lookups. To present day, many banking systems, insurange
companies, government agencies, etc still use COBOL because of how integral the language has been in legacy, foundational systems. It's
so uqiquittous and prevalent when it comes to banking that 95% of all daily ATM swipes and financial card transactions interact with
a COBOL background according to Gemini AI. COBOL handles trillions of dollars worth of transactions daily and the systems built COBOL require
maintenence from engineers that require anywhere from $150-300 hourly rate. The efforts to replace CODOL with a more modernized language may take
up to a decade and millions if not billions of dollors. 

SWIFT: the financial payment messaging Network
SWIFT founded in 1973 in Brussels, Belgium. SWIFT stands for the Society of Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication. SWIFT itself is not a bank but seems to be a
digital messaging system that securely communicates financial transactions, trade settlements, and treasury confirmations across an international network of banks. Just like COBOL programing,
SWIFT seems to also be integral to our modern banking systems as it was created in response to the collapse of the Bretton Woods system and the Nixon shock as
an alternative to relying on a single private U.S. bank's messaging network or manual Telex system according to SWIFT - Wikipedia. There are some 
compeititors that exist such as CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System), SPFS, BRICS Pay. Replacing SWIFT as of now if practically impossible due to the 
sear size of the network encompassing more than 11,000 institutions across the globe. This infrastructure would take tens of billions of dollars to begin restructuring
as well as the renegotiation of global policies. There are updates occuring with the technology, such as the generational migration from legacy FIN (MT) message formats 
to modern XML - based ISO 20022 (MX) standard. This transition is happening now and though the messaging technology coexist, soon the full adoption of modern XML ISO 20022  will
be required by all institutions within the SWIFT network and we'll no longer use the legacy messaging of FIN MT. I wonder, how Block Chain technology relates to SWIFT and its messaging systems.
